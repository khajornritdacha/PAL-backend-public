TOPIC 4: Consumer Behavior & the Buyer Decision Process
SMART Goal
By the end of this one-week course, I will be able to explain each stage of the buyer decision process model, identify the psychological, social, and cultural factors that influence purchase decisions, and apply behavioral frameworks to analyze a real marketing scenario — demonstrating this through a written case analysis of a product launch that examines how the brand targets consumers at each stage of the decision journey.
Mentor Discussion Summary
The student is a business undergraduate with basic marketing exposure but no prior coursework in consumer psychology. We agreed the course should anchor everything to the five-stage buyer decision model (need recognition, information search, evaluation of alternatives, purchase decision, post-purchase behavior) and treat all other concepts as forces acting on that model. Key psychological concepts to cover: motivation hierarchy, perception and selective attention, attitude formation, and cognitive dissonance after purchase. Social and cultural factors — reference groups, family roles, subculture — should be taught as layers that shape the same decision process rather than as a separate topic. I told the student that the real skill here is learning to ask "why did the consumer do that?" rather than memorizing frameworks. Every lesson should use a concrete brand example; Thai consumer behavior examples are preferred where available to maintain relevance. The course should end with a synthesis lesson connecting behavioral theory to actual marketing strategy decisions — segmentation, messaging, and channel selection. Keep abstract theory to a minimum; prioritize application.
