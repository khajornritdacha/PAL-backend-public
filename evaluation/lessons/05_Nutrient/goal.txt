TOPIC 3: Nutrition Science — Macronutrients, Metabolism & Dietary Guidelines
SMART Goal
By the end of this one-week course, I will be able to identify and distinguish the three macronutrients (carbohydrates, proteins, and fats), explain how each is metabolized for energy, interpret standard dietary guidelines including daily intake recommendations, and evaluate a real meal plan against those guidelines — demonstrating this through a written nutritional audit of a three-day personal diet log.
Mentor Discussion Summary
The student has casual interest in health and fitness but no formal biology or nutrition background. We agreed the course should start with what macronutrients are and why they matter, then move into metabolism at a conceptual level — no biochemical pathway detail, but enough to understand why different foods affect energy and body composition differently. Key concepts to cover: caloric density, glycemic index, nitrogen balance, basal metabolic rate, and the role of micronutrients as supporting context. I emphasized that dietary guidelines vary by organization and country, so the student should learn to read and compare sources critically rather than treating any single guideline as absolute truth. Visual aids should include food composition tables, metabolic flow diagrams, and the Thai RDNA alongside WHO guidelines for local relevance. The student's end goal is practical — understanding how to evaluate what they eat — so each lesson should close with a real-world application or example. Avoid diet culture framing; keep the tone neutral and evidence-based.
