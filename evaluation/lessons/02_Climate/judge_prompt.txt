CRITERIA RUBRIC

1. Multimedia Principle (d = 1.35) — words + pictures beat words alone

2. Redundancy Principle (d = 0.72) — graphics + narration beat graphics + narration + on-screen text
   a. Is on-screen text limited to keywords, short phrases, or labels (not full paragraphs)?
   b. Is the transcript substantively different from the on-screen text (elaborates, not verbatim)?
   c. Does the graphic — not the on-screen text — carry the primary informational weight?
   d. Does on-screen text avoid repeating what the graphic or narration already makes clear?
   e. Is total on-screen word count low enough to scan in roughly five seconds?

3. Modality Principle (d = 1.00) — graphics + narration beat graphics + on-screen text
   a. Are complex or detailed descriptions delivered via narration rather than dense on-screen text?
   b. Is the visual channel used primarily for graphics/diagrams rather than extended prose?
   c. Does the narration add information needed to fully understand the slide (slide is not self-sufficient as text alone)?
   d. Are there zero instances of the narration merely reading complex on-screen text aloud while a complex visual is shown?
   e. Is the narration written as natural, listenable spoken language (not transcribed bullet points)?

4. Coherence Principle (d = 0.86) — exclude the extraneous
   a. Are all images functional and relevant (no decorative clipart or stock filler)?
   b. Is the content free of tangential "fun facts," anecdotes, or details not serving the objective?
   c. Is the background clean and professional (no distracting patterns or busy backgrounds)?
   d. Is the narration free of filler, digressions, and off-topic content?
   e. Are fonts, colors, and styling consistent and non-distracting across slides?

5. Signaling Principle (d = 0.70) — cues emphasize key information
   a. Are there explicit visual cues (arrows, circles, highlights, color contrast, bold) marking key elements?
   b. Is there a clear visual hierarchy (titles, headers, sizing) organizing each slide?
   c. Does the narration use verbal signaling ("first," "notice," "the key idea is...") to direct attention?
   d. Do captions or callouts point the learner to the specific part of a visual being discussed?
   e. Is the single most important point of each slide clearly emphasized (centered, highlighted, or stated as the takeaway)?

6. Spatial Contiguity Principle (d = 1.10) — place related words and visuals near each other
   a. Are labels/captions placed physically near the part of the graphic they describe?
   b. Is text integrated into or adjacent to the graphic rather than isolated in a separate legend/key?
   c. Does the layout avoid split attention (related text not far from its image)?
   d. Are related items (caption + figure, term + diagram) grouped as a single unit?
   e. Is there a clear visual connection (proximity, leader line, matching color) between a term and its visual referent?
