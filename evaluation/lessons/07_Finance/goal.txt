TOPIC 5: Financial Statements & Ratio Analysis
SMART Goal
By the end of this one-week course, I will be able to read and interpret the three core financial statements (income statement, balance sheet, and cash flow statement), calculate and explain at least eight key financial ratios across liquidity, profitability, and solvency categories, and use those ratios to assess the financial health of a real company — demonstrating this through a written ratio analysis report based on a publicly available annual report.
Mentor Discussion Summary
The student has basic accounting awareness but has never formally worked with financial statements. We agreed the course must first build intuition for what each statement is measuring and why it exists before introducing any numbers — students who jump straight to ratios without understanding the underlying statements make systematic interpretation errors. The income statement should come first, then the balance sheet, then cash flow, with one lesson dedicated to how the three link together. Ratio analysis should be taught in clusters by category, not as an undifferentiated list. Key ratios to cover: current ratio, quick ratio, gross and net profit margin, ROE, ROA, debt-to-equity, and interest coverage. I stressed that ratios are only meaningful in context — the student must learn to compare against industry benchmarks and prior periods, not evaluate numbers in isolation. Visual aids should include annotated statement layouts and ratio formula cards with worked examples. Use a Thai-listed company's financials if possible to keep sourcing accessible. Avoid deep accounting theory; the goal is financial literacy for decision-making, not preparation for an accounting exam.
