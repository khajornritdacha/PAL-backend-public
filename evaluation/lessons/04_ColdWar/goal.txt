TOPIC 1: The Cold War — Origins, Proxy Conflicts & Legacy
SMART Goal
By the end of this one-week course, I will be able to explain the ideological and geopolitical origins of the Cold War, identify and analyze at least four major proxy conflicts (Korean War, Vietnam War, Cuban Missile Crisis, and Afghanistan), and evaluate the Cold War's lasting legacy on current international relations — demonstrating this through a written comparative analysis of two proxy conflicts and a short reflection on a post-Cold War geopolitical issue.
Mentor Discussion Summary
The student has a basic understanding of 20th-century history but limited exposure to Cold War specifics. We agreed the course should follow a chronological structure in three phases: origins (1945–1950), escalation and proxy conflicts (1950–1979), and decline and legacy (1980–present). I emphasized that the student should not treat this as a list of events — the focus is on cause-and-effect logic: why did each superpower act as it did, and what were the structural outcomes. Key concepts to anchor each lesson: containment doctrine, the domino theory, détente, and the arms race. Visuals should include maps showing spheres of influence and timelines linking events. The student is particularly interested in how Cold War dynamics explain present-day US-Russia and US-China tensions, so each section should connect backward to contemporary relevance. Avoid excessive detail on military tactics; prioritize political decision-making and ideological framing.
